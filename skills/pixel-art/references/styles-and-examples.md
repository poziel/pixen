## Style vocabulary for discussion

This is a working classification, not an exhaustive or historically definitive taxonomy. Categories overlap. Labels describe choices, not quality rankings.

| Visual approach | Typical choices | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monochrome / 1-bit | Two tones; shape and negative space carry information | Are important objects distinguishable without extra colors? |
| Limited-palette retro | Shared colors and constrained ramps | Does each color contribute useful separation? |
| Minimalist / very low resolution | Strong abstraction and few defining marks | Does the intended identity survive reduction? |
| Outlined cartoon | Contour and exaggerated forms | Do contours clarify rather than crowd small features? |
| Clean cluster rendering | Broad controlled color masses, selective detail | Do cluster boundaries describe the intended surfaces? |
| Detailed arcade-inspired | Rich forms, materials and expressive poses | Does detail support action readability? |
| Painterly pixel art | Softer transitions and nuanced color relationships | Are forms and edges still deliberately resolved? |
| Dither-heavy rendering | Structured patterns for tone and texture | Does the texture help at the actual display size? |
| Anime-inspired | Stylized facial proportions, expressions and hair masses | Does identity remain consistent between body and portrait? |
| Hybrid / modern presentation | Pixel sprites combined with other rendering treatments | Is the combination coherent and intentional? |

Some rows are categories to populate with further verified examples, not claims that the current sources fully cover them. Use the vocabulary to describe choices rather than to classify all artwork into rigid boxes.

## Keep independent dimensions separate

- **Projection:** side view, top-down/three-quarter, isometric-style, perspective.
- **Rendering:** outline behavior, shading, texture, palette and detail density.
- **Proportions:** realistic, heroic, chibi, abstract or species-specific.
- **Theme:** fantasy, science fiction, horror, cozy, industrial and others.
- **Presentation:** source density, display scale, lighting effects and motion treatment.

A fantasy game can use minimalist monochrome art or highly detailed painterly art. Isometric is a projection choice, not a guarantee of a particular palette or genre. SLYNYRD's isometric lesson explains that the familiar pixel-grid 2:1 slope approximates rather than exactly reproduces true isometric angles.

## Add your references

For each artwork, capture: artist; original URL; image or section; asset type; what you enjoy; what is technically effective; what you would avoid copying; intended use as inspiration or a match target; and review decision. Example: “I like the muted colors, but the transferable craft lesson is that the character stays distinct from the scenery.”
