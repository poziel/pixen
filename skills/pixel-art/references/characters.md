# Characters, creatures and portraits

Human references: F1, F2, C1, C2 and S2 in [sources](sources.md). Anatomy, contact and identity checks below are synthesis, not claims that the tutorials cover every species or equipment type.

## Establish an identity that survives the grid

Choose a few defining features: silhouette, proportions, head shape, costume masses or a characteristic object. Block body parts in flat colors. Check balance, facing and negative space between limbs before adding costume texture.

Use the brief's proportions. A large head can help expression on a tiny sprite, but is not required for every character. A mask, helmet, hood or nonhuman face can carry identity. Do not force every asset into the same exposed human-face template.

## Construct the pose

Think of the head, ribcage, pelvis and limbs as volumes with consistent orientation. Establish ground contact and weight. Make overlapping forms distinguishable at display size. For held equipment, inspect the hand and grip together: fingers/contact, handle direction and arm posture must agree. Detail cannot repair a disconnected weapon or impossible joint.

Check facing directions together. Mirror only when lighting and asymmetrical costume/equipment remain correct. Reuse a body base when it supports consistency, but preserve differences in build, pose and identity.

## Creatures

Begin with species-specific structure: body mass, limb placement, head attachment and locomotion. Preserve anatomical appendages in the full silhouette. Compare relative sizes in the same scene; a small creature should not become enormous merely because it was fitted into a preview rectangle. Natural reference is useful for structure even when rendering is highly stylized.

## Portraits

Use a source with enough information for the desired expression and identity. Block head, hair/headwear and neck, then place facial landmarks consistently with the head's orientation. Check eye spacing, mouth position and the readability of the expression at its actual slot size.

A tiny sprite-face enlargement adds no new detail. Distinguish an exact crop, a cleaned/resized source crop and separately drawn matching art. Keep defining masks or headwear; remove unintended equipment fragments only when required by the framing/brief. Preserve source provenance.

## Useful corrections

- If the pose disappears under clothing, return to flat body masses.
- If the character feels stiff, revisit weight and orientation before adding frames or fabric folds.
- If face highlights fragment the expression, simplify them around the facial structure.
- If roster identities blur together, compare silhouettes and major color masses side by side.

Inspect the complete body, grip contact, intended portrait framing and a representative scene. File dimensions alone do not prove good anatomy or character design.
